# June Mercer: how she looks and who she is

Agreed on 12 November 2026. Use this for every image and video of June, so that she stays the same woman. The reference picture is still to be chosen: the prompts below are the second attempt.

Everyone in the book calls her "Nora" or "Miss Bell".

## Description

- **Age:** 33. She looks about 30.
- **Build:** 5'6", slim, with a waitress's strong forearms and red, chapped hands.
- **Face:** oval, with high cheekbones, a straight nose, a full mouth and a strong jaw. The jaw is Amos's.
- **Eyes:** pale blue, like Amos's. Light shadows under them after the bus ride.
- **Hair:** shoulder-length honey blonde, bleached at home, with an inch of dark brown roots. In a low ponytail when she travels or works.
- **Skin and teeth:** clear, even skin. Even, straight teeth. (The real Nora had a mole on her cheek and a crooked front tooth.)
- **Makeup:** a bare face. She is beautiful without trying.
- **Expression at rest:** calm and watchful, with a small dry lift at one corner of the mouth.
- **Clothes, chapters 1 to 19:** a thin camel thrift-store coat a size too big, a grey sweater, dark jeans, plain black work sneakers. Drugstore reading glasses that cost three dollars. Without them she cannot read print. They vanish from beside her bed in chapter 8.
- **Her suitcase:** small, cheap, soft-sided, navy blue, scuffed, with a broken zipper pull. It is the suitcase that later stands packed by the front door.
- **Her phone:** a cheap prepaid one from a drugstore, with a few dollars of minutes left. It has no signal at the farmhouse (chapter 6).
- **From chapter 20:** Amos's brown canvas barn coat with the corduroy collar, every day.

## Personality

Dry and funny on the inside, quiet and watchful on the outside.

- **Outside:** polite and still, with few words. She has a waitress's smile that she can switch on for a customer. When she is afraid she goes quiet.
- **Inside:** she notices everything and counts everything: tables, tips, exits, what a coat cost. She judges people fast and is usually right. Her humour is dry and often aimed at herself.
- **The wound:** a charming man with paperwork cost her three years, so she distrusts kindness, most of all from pleasant men. She was given away as a baby, so she does not expect anyone to keep her.
- **What she wants:** a place where she belongs. She would never say so.
- **Faults:** she is proud and will not ask for help. She lies smoothly and hates herself for it. In Part Three the money really tempts her.
- **Habits:** she reads every paper before she signs it. She sleeps lightly. She is half a second slow to answer to "Nora".
- **How she changes:** she starts alone, under a borrowed name, trusting nobody. She ends by trusting Wade and Teddy, taking the blame herself, and signing her own name.

She smiles rarely, so the one laugh Julian gets out of her in chapter 2 counts.

## Her voice on the page: what works

Added on 15 November 2026, after the Kimi K3 reading of the prologue and chapters 1 to 3. That reader called her voice "the strongest part of the reading experience". These are the things it praised. Keep doing them in every chapter.

- **She counts money to the dollar.** What she has, what a thing costs, and what she takes instead. Forty-one dollars. The roast beef at $11.95, and the seven-dollar grilled cheese she orders. The eleven-dollar box of hair dye. Give at least one exact sum in every chapter while she is poor.
- **Her hopes are small.** She hopes for "a few thousand dollars", which to her means one month of not counting. This is why the reader believes her, and it is why the big money will feel big.
- **She prices people.** She reads a person by what they wear and by what is missing: the pale stripe where Sam's watch was, the store creases in Julian's coat, his clean boots, Colleen's real bag and watch. One or two things for each person, never a list.
- **She knows when she is being priced.** She can tell one kind of look from another: looked at as a woman, as a stranger, as a suspect, as something with a price on it.
- **She reads a room like a waitress.** Her comparisons come from tables, checks, tips and customers: "the customer who sends the soup back", and the family that fights through dinner and then blames the waitress.
- **She is honest about what she sees and silent about who she is.** The reader said: "I trust her observations, but I do not completely trust her." That is where we want the reader to stand.
- **She says little and thinks a lot.** Other people talk. She answers in a few words, and the reader hears the rest.
- **Her jokes are dry and aimed at herself**, and they sit right beside the fear.
- **She works people out before they work her out.** In the reader's words, she "has survived by learning how to calculate people before they can calculate her".

### Things to watch

- **Every comparison must explain itself.** "A table of eight at closing time" was not clear to a reader whose first language is not English. Say the plain thing: "customers who walk in a minute before we close".
- **No new hints about her past until chapter 13.** In three chapters she has hinted at the cologne, the paperwork, the arithmetic job, the dead roommate, signatures and keys. The reader said this begins to feel like "a collection of teasers". From now on a chapter may bring back an old hint. It does not add a new one.
- **She does not point at her name.** The reader already asks why signing makes her nervous, and uses the word "identity". She never says "my name", and she should not keep saying "the name on the envelope" either. Write the sentence so that no name is needed.
- **She does not explain what she has just shown.** If the stripe on Sam's wrist has told us he is broke, she does not say it again. Keep an explanation only where a plain reader would be lost without it.
- **About three one-line jokes in a chapter**, and no more.

## Her money

She counts it, so we must count it too.

- **On the bus:** forty-one dollars: a twenty, a ten, a five and six ones.
- **After the diner (chapter 2):** forty dollars. Julian paid for her supper, and she left one dollar for Pat.
- **Chapters 3 to 7:** forty dollars. She spends nothing on the page.
- **Who pays for what:** the estate paid for her bus ticket and pays for her motel bed. It does not pay for her meals. Once she lives on the farm she has a room and a monthly allowance from the estate.

## Effects on the story

- **The bleached hair.** She went blonde six years ago, when she took the name, so that she would look less like her prison photograph. She never tells the reader why. Nora was dark, and June's natural colour is dark too, which is why the clinic staff confused them.
- **Chapter 3.** Wade looks at a pretty blonde and says "mistress". Colleen looks at her roots and agrees.
- **Chapter 5.** Through the gap of the chained door, Mrs. Pruitt's eye stops at her dark roots and then stays on her face for a long time.
- **Chapter 11.** Ruth says she has "his stubborn jaw". The pale blue eyes are Amos's as well. Both are honest clues that she is his blood.
- **Chapter 39.** In the prison photograph she is 23 and dark-haired.
