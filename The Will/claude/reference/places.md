# The Will: places and the journey

Proposed on 12 November 2026. Use this for every chapter and every image, so that the geography stays the same. Marlow, Tolliver and Tolliver County are invented. Milwaukee, Chicago, Indianapolis, Vincennes and the Wabash River are real.

## The three places

| Place | What it is | What happens there |
|---|---|---|
| **Marlow** | A small town, about 1,900 people. One main street on the old highway. | Everyday life: the bus stop, the motel, the diner, the tavern, Lang's office, Teddy's flat. |
| **Tolliver** | The county town, about 9,000 people, 25 minutes beyond Marlow. | Official life: the courthouse, the judge, the sheriff's office, the hospital, the laboratory envelope. |
| **The Hale farm** | 1,200 flat acres by the river, 25 to 30 minutes from Marlow. | Everything else. |

In the story file, "the town" means Marlow and "the county town" means Tolliver.

## Marlow

- Flat southwest Indiana, near the Wabash River. Eastern time.
- **The gas station** at the edge of town. The bus stops here once a day in each direction.
- **The Marlow Motor Inn**: one storey, twelve rooms, a parking lot, an office with a bell. Chapter 1 ends here.
- **Lottie's**, the diner: on Main Street, the only hot food after six (chapter 2). Steamed-up windows, a menu board on the wall, a counter with a register, one waitress, Pat. From its window you can see Lang's street door (chapter 5).
- **The tavern**: where Teddy's car stands every night. Name to be agreed later.
- **Lang's office**: on Main Street, upstairs, beside the bank. A street door with a brass plate and a buzzer, a very small elevator, one floor up (chapter 3). One lighted window at night (chapter 37).
- Also: a grain elevator by the railway line, a church, a small grocery that closes at six.
- **Teddy** rents a flat here.

## Tolliver

- The courthouse with the probate judge (chapters 19 and 23), and the café where June sees Julian with Colleen.
- The sheriff's office and the cells (chapter 58).
- The county hospital that kept Amos's tissue sample.
- Lang spent the whole day of Amos's death here, in court.

## The Hale farm

- From Marlow: 25 to 30 minutes on county roads, then **the lane**, two miles long, which leads only to the farm.
- **The farmhouse**, with Amos's study below June's room, and the steep cellar stairs.
- **Wade's small house** at the far end of the farm.
- **The river field**, beside the river, with the old well at its edge. Amos died here.
- A main power line crosses one corner of the land.
- Neighbours: **Ruth Tanner**, and the house that **Julian's** company rents for him. Nobody else is near. (Where Julian's house stands is open: chapters 1 and 5 say there is nothing on the river road but Ruth's place and Hale Lane.)
- It is too far to walk to town. June uses **Amos's old pickup**, which belongs to the estate. Mrs. Pruitt cannot drive.

## How the farm lies

Put together on 17 November 2026 from what chapters 1 to 7 already say. Nothing here is new. Use it for the view from June's window in chapter 8.

- **The river road** runs from Marlow toward the river, twenty-five minutes. County bus 897 leaves the gas station at 12:12 every day and goes out along it. There is nothing on this road but Ruth Tanner's place and Hale Lane.
- **Hale Lane** starts at a mailbox with HALE painted on it by hand. There is no sign and no gate. It is two miles of gravel between two fences, and it leads only to the farm.
- **The river field** is on the left of the lane as you drive in, and it runs all the way down to the river. Its gate is on the lane: Teddy's car stood there, on the gravel. The one plowed strip is along its edge, the fence is at the bottom, and the old well is at its edge.
- **The house** is white and stands on a little rise at the end of the lane, with the yard in front of it. The porch faces the yard. The river side of the house looks down the slope at the river field: the kitchen window over the sink, and June's window above the study. From there you see a person in the field, but not a face.
- **Wade's house** is small, with his truck beside it, far off at the other end of the fields. The land is flat, so from his kitchen he can see the porch of the farmhouse and its lighted windows.
- **The barn and the old tractor:** near the house. Their exact place is fixed in chapter 8.
- **No phone signal at the farmhouse** (chapter 6). The black telephone under the stairs is the only line out of the house. There is a signal at Ruth Tanner's place and under the power line by the river, and it comes and goes with the day (decided on 17 November).

## Inside the farmhouse

Fixed by chapters 6 and 7 on 16 November 2026. It is an old farmhouse, about 120 years old.

- **Outside.** A white house on a rise, with a front porch, a screen door and a wooden door. The front door has a new brass chain, which goes on at dark.
- **The hallway.** The front door opens into a narrow hallway, not into a room. It is dark, brown and cold, and it smells of floor wax. The staircase goes straight up from it. Amos's barn coat hangs on the hooks by the door. Under the stairs is a little table with the only telephone, black, which Mrs. Pruitt answers.
- **The living room** ("the front room"): through a wide doorway on the right of the hallway. It has not been used since Mrs. Hale died. Every chair, the sofa and something tall in the corner are under white sheets.
- **Mr. Hale's study:** the one door on the left of the hallway. It has the only lock inside the house, a big black iron one, and it is always locked. Mrs. Pruitt carries the one key on the ring in her apron pocket. Inside are his desk, a black stove, a brown rug and his papers. It is under June's room.
- **The kitchen:** at the far end of the hallway, at the back of the house, and the only warm room. A big black stove, a long table, one chair with arms at the head of it and plain chairs along the sides. A back door, with the feed-store calendar and its pencil beside it. The window over the sink looks down the slope at the river field. It has a curtain.
- **The cellar:** through a narrow door in the corner of the kitchen, kept shut with an iron hook. The stairs are as steep as a ladder and have no rail. Decided on 16 November, and not yet in the text: the cellar has a second way in, two slanted wooden doors outside, behind the house.
- **Mrs. Pruitt's room:** through a door beside the kitchen stove.
- **The stairs:** thirteen steps. The ninth cracks like a gunshot when you stand on it.
- **Upstairs:** a hallway with five shut doors, the family's bedrooms. No bedroom has a key.
- **June's room:** the last door, at the far end, over the study. It was Mrs. Hale's. Narrow, with one window, an iron bed, a small table, a chair, a rag rug and a cold radiator. The window looks down the slope at the river field and the old well (to be shown in chapter 8), with Wade's house far beyond.
- **The grate:** a square hole in the floor of June's room, between the bed and the wall, the size of a serving tray, with an iron grate over it. It is rusted open. Through it June sees the top of the study stove, a piece of the rug and one corner of the desk, and she hears every sound from the study. Sound goes both ways.
- **Under June's bed:** Mrs. Hale's suitcase, packed forty-one years ago.

## Other places

- **Milwaukee, Wisconsin**: where June has lived as Nora Bell and worked as a waitress. Amos meant to "drive up" there.
- **Vincennes**: "the next town", about 35 minutes from Marlow. The Friday dinner with Julian (chapter 16).
- **Lang's fishing cabin**: four hours' drive to the north.
- **The city where Colleen lives**: three hours away (Indianapolis).

## June's journey in chapter 1

By car, Milwaukee to Marlow is about seven hours. By bus it takes all day, because she changes twice and the last bus stops in every small town.

1. **Milwaukee to Chicago**: Greyhound. She leaves at about 5:00 in the morning.
2. **Chicago to Indianapolis**: Greyhound.
3. **Indianapolis to Marlow**: a regional bus on the Indianapolis–Evansville line (in real life, Miller Transportation's "Hoosier Ride"). One bus a day. It stops at the gas station.

She steps off in Marlow at **6:15 in the evening**. It is October and the light is going. About twelve hours door to door: eleven on the road and one waiting.

- 6:15 PM: off the bus.
- 6:30 PM: the motel. The clerk: "Hale. So you're the one."
- 7:00 PM: the diner (chapter 2).
- Next morning, 10:00 AM: Lang's office (chapter 3).

Lang's office sent the bus ticket and paid for one night at the motel. Meals are not covered.
