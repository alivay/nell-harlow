**A note on chapter numbers (17 November 2026).** This file is a diary. Each reading keeps the chapter numbers of the day it was written. Chapter 9 onward keep their numbers.

## How to use a report

- These readers are sharper than most people. They remember every word of the prologue. If they are fooled, a human reader will be fooled too.
- A wrong guess that matches our plan for that chapter is a success.
- A guess that reaches the truth too early is a problem, and it needs a fix.
- After a fix, test again in a fresh thread, so that the reader does not remember the old draft or its old theory.

### Problem A: the reader puts Teddy at the body in chapter 3

**Why it happened.** The prologue alone did not point at anybody. The same reader said the voice "may be guilty of murder, guilty only of silence, or guilty of having wanted Amos dead". Chapter 3 pointed at Teddy. It drew the three children as three types, and only one type fits the voice in the field:

- Teddy's hand "isn't quite steady". The voice in the field hides its shaking hands.
- Teddy's face is red, his voice cracks, and he may have slept in his jacket. The voice in the field is frightened and ashamed.
- Wade says "the whole county knows why". The voice says "half the county heard me".
- Teddy lists the tractor's faults. So does the voice.
- Wade laughs and accuses. Colleen is ice. Neither sounds like the voice.
- Wade's joke that Colleen "would need a map to find the barn" tells the reader she was never in that field.

Once the reader had chosen Teddy, the rest followed: Teddy is weak, weak people do not murder, so he only found the body.

**The rule we broke.** `planted-clues.md` already said it: in chapter 3 the reader should be able to try the prologue voice on each of the three children.

**Decided on 15 November, and done in chapter 3, draft 3.** Aviel added the opening of the quarrel: Colleen asks why Wade is on his feet, whether he will hit her, "or would you like to kill me too?"

1. **Teddy is calm and kind**, as Aviel first pictured him. No shaking hand, no red face, no cracking voice. He is the only Hale who looks as if he slept. He tries to stop the fight with a joke. His weakness stays hidden until chapter 21. Then the reader thinks: the nice one was standing over the body all along.
2. **Colleen is the one shouting through the door**, at Wade. Behind the door she is furious. When the door opens she is ice again, in one second.
3. **Colleen says that Wade found him**, at four in the afternoon. The man who "found" the body is the reader's first choice for the voice in the field.
4. **Wade answers that Colleen came home twice in the last week**, after years of Christmas cards. So she was near the farm, and she had a quarrel of her own.
5. **Each one gets a detail against the type.** Wade's laugh is big and empty, and he has not slept in a month. Colleen has bitten one thumbnail to the quick. Teddy is rested and easy.
6. **Wade's joke about the map goes.**
7. **June hears "eighty million dollars" through the door**, while she is still hoping for a few thousand. This answers the open question of where she hears the number.
8. **Cut "the name from my envelope" in the elevator.** One less finger pointing at her name.

**New facts this adds to the story**, if agreed: Colleen drove down twice in the last week of her father's life. Two facts are already in the story file and would now reach the reader early: Wade found the body at four in the afternoon, and all three children quarrelled with their father that week.

**For later chapters:**

- Chapter 4: Lang can say, dryly, that in his last week Amos quarrelled with all three of his children. It spreads the suspicion over the three, and it is the killer who spreads it.
- The exact words "dance on your grave" are kept back until the funeral in chapter 36. That is where the book wants the reader to decide that Teddy is the voice.

### Problem B: the prologue tells the reader what time it is

This is not in the reader's report. It follows from it. Kimi has not read chapter 4 yet, so this is a prediction.

**The danger.**

- The prologue says it is dawn: "the sky is going gray", "in a few minutes it will be light enough to see a face", "he had only just started this morning".
- The rest of the book says he died in the afternoon. Wade found him at four. Lang was told at five.
- In chapter 4, as planned, Lang says: "I sat with your father at first light on the very day he died."
- All three readings so far doubted that the voice in the field is the killer: "probably framed", 7%, "found him already dead".

A reader who believes that the voice only found the body at dawn will ask who was in that field before. In chapter 4 the lawyer answers the question himself. That reader is looking at the killer forty-six chapters too early.

There is a second cost. The reader knows from page one that the afternoon story is false. So the eleven hours on the hour meter (chapters 48 and 49) surprise June, and they do not surprise the reader.

**Decided on 15 November, and done in prologue, draft 4.** Take the time of day out of the prologue. Nothing false goes in. It is still dawn, and every line that stays is true at dawn.

- "He had only just started this morning" becomes "He had only just started".
- The gray sky, the growing light and the one lit window come out. In their place: the kitchen window looks straight down into the field, and from up there you can see a person but not a face.
- "In this light whoever stands beside his tractor is him" becomes "whoever stands beside his tractor is him".

The reader will then place the scene in the afternoon, shortly before Wade "found" him. Lang's visit at first light looks like something that happened hours before the death.

**What we gain.**

- Lang's sentence in chapter 4 hides in plain sight for the reader too.
- The eleven hours become a real surprise, and they send the reader back to the prologue.
- The dawn itself is not lost. The gray sky and the light coming go to chapter 50, where Teddy tells it, and to chapter 54, where Lang remembers it. There they are the twist.

**What it costs.** The prologue loses its sunrise. Aviel wanted that picture, so this is his decision.

**If agreed, watch these:**

- Until chapter 48, June does not say "morning" or "dawn" about the death. In chapters 9 and 21 she says the tractor looks "as Wade found it".
- Mrs. Pruitt's sentence in chapter 31, "the morning Mr. Hale died", stays. It is the first crack.
- Mrs. Pruitt called to "Mr. Hale" by his tractor at first light. Nobody tells June this before chapter 50, because it would give the reader the time of the prologue.
- The tractor's lights were still on when Wade found it at four in the afternoon. That can be a clue in the sheriff's photograph in chapter 48.
- Any picture or video of the prologue must be gray and wet, with no sun and no lit window.

### Problem D: the reader doubts that she is the granddaughter

"I finished Chapter 4 unsure whether Nora is Amos's granddaughter at all." It predicts that she is not Carol's daughter, or that somebody will falsify the test.

Why: the lie to Lang ("None of that happened"), which it called "the book's best trick", and the old last line.

This is close to what June herself believes, and the story file wanted the reader to think her fear was only about being poor and alone. But the reader has not guessed the real secret (she is another woman under Nora's name), and it has not guessed the truth behind that (she is the granddaughter after all). The doubt is what makes the DNA test a cliffhanger.

**Decided on 15 November (Aviel).** There are two secrets, and they are treated differently.

1. **"She may not be Amos's granddaughter."** The reader is allowed to suspect this from chapter 4. June believes it herself. It is the question of Part One, and it gives her fear of the test a plain reason. So chapter 4 ends with June saying plainly that the test will take the money away: "One test, and it's all gone." In chapters 23 and 24 the test says yes, and the reader is confused: then why is she still so frightened? Chapter 25 answers.
2. **"She is not Nora Bell."** This is the twist of chapter 25 ("My name is not Nora Bell"). It must stay hidden. June never thinks "Nora" or "June" about herself, and she never says "the real Nora".

Keep the lie about the two visits. It is Aviel's idea and the reader's favorite moment.

**Aviel's point (15 November).** A plain reader assumes that Nora Bell is the granddaughter. So for that reader "she is not his granddaughter" quickly becomes "she is not Nora Bell". The ending must therefore never say that she is lying about who she is. The first try, "I can't lie to a DNA test", did say it, and it was cut.

**The ending now (Aviel's version).** It is about loss. For one minute she had eighty million dollars. She did not care whether she was really the granddaughter: the lawyer said so, and she was not going to argue. She meant to live her year, take the money and go. The test will take it all away, and it proves what she always knew: there is no free money. This leaves the reader an easy explanation for her fear: the lawyer may be wrong.

**Watch in the next report:** a reader who says "she is not his granddaughter" is fine. A reader who says "she is not Nora", "she took somebody's name" or "the roommate is the real Nora" has guessed too early.
